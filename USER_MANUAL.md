# User Manual — GxP AI Readiness & Governance Assessment

## 1. Purpose
This tool provides a structured, PM-oriented initial readiness assessment for AI initiatives in Life Sciences.

It helps identify potential gaps before pilot, validation, deployment or lifecycle stages.

**Important:** It is not a regulatory compliance certification and does not replace qualified QA, Validation, Regulatory, Clinical, Medical, Manufacturing, Pharmacovigilance, Data, Privacy, Security or business review.

## 2. Who should use it
- Project Managers and Program Managers
- Life Sciences transformation leaders
- AI product or business owners
- PMO and governance teams
- Teams preparing an AI use case for formal QA / validation / regulatory review

## 3. How to use the tool

### Step 1 — Enter the AI project
Enter:
- AI project / use case name
- Business objective
- Primary AI use type
- Impact on regulated decisions

Use a specific description. Example: **AI-assisted Deviation Triage**.

### Step 2 — Select the primary GxP / regulated domain
Choose the best-fit context:
- GMP — Good Manufacturing Practice
- GLP — Good Laboratory Practice
- GCP — Good Clinical Practice
- GDP — Good Distribution Practice
- PV / GVP — Pharmacovigilance / Good Pharmacovigilance Practices
- GDocP / Data Integrity
- CSV / CSA
- Multiple / Cross-functional GxP
- Not yet determined

For cross-functional solutions, select **Multiple / Cross-functional GxP** and confirm the scope with the relevant stakeholders.

### Step 3 — Answer the readiness questions
Use:
- **Yes:** the control/capability is defined and evidenced.
- **Partly:** work exists but is incomplete, draft, or not yet fully approved.
- **No:** it has not yet been established.

Answer based on evidence rather than choosing responses to increase the score.

### Step 4 — Review the common governance areas
The assessment covers:
1. Context of Use & Governance
2. Data Governance & Data Integrity
3. AI Model & Validation / Assurance Readiness
4. Human Oversight & Decision Governance
5. CSV / CSA & Quality Controls
6. Lifecycle Management
7. Program & Organizational Readiness

### Step 5 — Rate delivery risk
Rate:
- Schedule risk
- Dependency risk
- Resource risk

Use a higher rating where a credible delivery concern exists.

## 4. Understanding the score
The tool produces an Overall Readiness score:

| Score | Status | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 80–100% | Green | Relatively strong readiness; continue with normal governance and qualified review. |
| 60–79% | Amber | Material gaps remain; use a controlled gap-closure plan before the next stage. |
| Below 60% | Red | Significant gaps exist; do not treat the assessment as ready for pilot or production. |

The score is a **decision-support indicator**, not a compliance determination.

The calculation gives additional weight to GxP/domain, data governance and validation/quality areas. High delivery risk can also reduce the overall result.

## 5. How to interpret results
Example:

**72% — Amber**

This means the assessment indicates a workable foundation but material gaps should be addressed before progressing.

Review the lowest-scoring areas first. Those are the areas most likely to need PM attention and stakeholder decisions.

## 6. Recommended use across the project lifecycle

### Project initiation
Run a baseline assessment and capture the key gaps.

### Before pilot
Reassess after the highest-priority gaps have been addressed.

### Before validation / release
Confirm that quality, validation, data, human oversight and change controls have been reviewed by the appropriate qualified stakeholders.

### During lifecycle management
Reassess when:
- Context of use changes
- Data sources change
- The model changes
- Deployment context changes
- Performance deteriorates
- A new regulated process is introduced

## 7. Example
**Use case:** AI-assisted deviation triage in a GMP quality environment.

Possible gaps:
- Human override not clearly defined
- Data lineage incomplete
- Validation ownership not established
- Model-change triggers not defined

Possible governance outcome:

> Proceed to controlled pilot planning only after closure of critical data, validation and human-oversight gaps.

## 8. Assessment summary
After completing the questionnaire, select **Generate Assessment Summary**.

The summary can support:
- Project governance discussions
- RAID/action tracking
- Steering-committee reviews
- Gap-closure planning
- Future reassessments

It should not replace controlled project, quality, validation or regulatory documentation.

## 9. Good governance practices
- Keep the **Context of Use** specific.
- Match controls to risk and intended use.
- Make human accountability explicit.
- Document data lineage and data-quality considerations.
- Define validation / assurance responsibility early.
- Plan for model and data changes before production.
- Involve relevant stakeholders early.
- Use the assessment to expose gaps, not to make a project “pass.”

## 10. What the tool does not do
It does not:
- Declare GMP, GLP, GCP, GDP or GVP compliance
- Approve a validation strategy
- Provide legal advice
- Replace formal risk, quality, validation or regulatory assessments
- Decide whether an AI model is fit for a regulated purpose

## 11. Suggested participants
Depending on the use case, involve the relevant:
- Business / process owner
- Project / program manager
- QA
- Validation / CSV / CSA
- Regulatory
- Data / Data Science
- IT / Engineering
- Privacy / Security
- Clinical / Medical
- Manufacturing / Quality Control
- Pharmacovigilance
- Supplier / vendor management

## 12. Portfolio / demonstration positioning
This application demonstrates:
- Life Sciences program governance

- Risk-based thinking
- AI project readiness assessment
- AI-enabled project decision support

It should be presented as a **prototype / decision-support tool**, not as a regulatory product.
